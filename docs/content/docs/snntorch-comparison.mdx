We compare snnTorch and SNNLab's native `snnlab.sim` training on a matched MNIST
classifier, measuring learning curves and execution time.

## Task and architecture

Both networks use **784 inputs → 1,000 spiking hidden neurons → 10 spiking
outputs**. They train on **6,000 stratified MNIST images** and evaluate all
**10,000 test images**: 20 epochs, batch size 128, seed 17 and 940 updates.
Each image repeats for 25 steps with fresh state; the largest output spike count
predicts the digit.

snnTorch uses `Linear` and `snn.Synaptic`; SNNLab matches them with exponential
currents, a custom neuron and bias inputs. Both learn 795,010 parameters.

| Shared setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Synaptic decay per step, alpha | 0.9 |
| Membrane decay per step, beta | 0.95 |
| Spike threshold | 1, in normalized neuron units |
| Reset | Delayed subtractive reset, detached from the gradient |
| Surrogate gradient | snnTorch's default ATan derivative, matched in SNNLab |
| Optimizer | Adam, learning rate 0.0005, betas (0.9, 0.999), no weight decay or gradient clipping |
| Training objective | Batch-mean cross-entropy on output membrane voltages, summed over all 25 steps |

Both share initial weights, shuffled order, float32 and deterministic arithmetic,
with TF32 disabled. SNNLab uses fast projections, eager callbacks and **`TrainSpec`,
`ExecutionSpec` and one `train()` call**; snnTorch uses a PyTorch loop.

This adapts [snnTorch Tutorial 5](https://snntorch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/tutorial_5.html)
with synaptic decay and SNNLab's [training workflow](/training). The custom reset
matches snnTorch rather than built-in `CUBA_LIF`. Preflight checks compare outputs,
losses, gradients and an Adam update.

## Training curves

**Figure 1.** Both networks were trained on the same 6,000 MNIST images for 20
epochs. Panel A shows training-split cross-entropy, summed over the 25 simulation
steps. Panel B shows spike-count classification accuracy on all 10,000 test
images. Each point evaluates fixed weights after its epoch; the training loss is
not a running average collected while weights change. Black solid lines denote
snnTorch and red dashed lines denote SNNLab. The lines are unsmoothed results from
one seed, without uncertainty bands.

Final test accuracy is **85.20% for snnTorch and 80.01% for SNNLab**. This single
seed does not establish training equivalence or a general accuracy ranking.
Timing instrumentation left both learning histories unchanged.

## Execution timings

Training used **two Modal L4 GPUs concurrently**, excluding data preparation,
warmup, disk writes, plotting and volume commits from both timers. Isolated
passes used **one L4**, randomized interleaving, five warmups and three shared
weight states. Their pooled medians cover 150 samples per library/operation,
each processing **128 images × 25 steps**.

| Measurement | snnTorch | SNNLab | SNNLab speed advantage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Total workflow | 162.39 s | 148.61 s | +9.3% |
| Workflow per epoch | 8.12 s | 7.43 s | +9.3% |
| Training only | 59.94 s | 53.46 s | +12.1% |
| Training per epoch | 3.00 s | 2.67 s | +12.1% |
| Train/test evaluations, including initial | 102.42 s | 95.04 s | +7.8% |
| Evaluations per completed epoch | 4.88 s | 4.52 s | +7.8% |
| Isolated training forward | 27.60 ms | 21.86 ms | +26.2% |
| Isolated backward | 14.09 ms | 14.53 ms | -3.1% |
| Isolated inference forward | 25.75 ms | 23.64 ms | +8.9% |

1. **Total workflow:** setup, training, initial/epoch evaluations and in-memory checkpoints.
2. **Workflow per epoch:** total divided by 20, including setup and initial evaluation.
3. **Training only:** all 940 updates: batch preparation, forward/loss, backward, optimizer and bookkeeping; excludes evaluation.
4. **Training per epoch:** training-only time divided by 20.
5. **Train/test evaluations, including initial:** fixed-weight evaluations before and after training epochs.
6. **Evaluations per completed epoch:** post-epoch evaluation time divided by 20; excludes initial evaluation.
7. **Isolated training forward:** fresh-state forward with autograd; excludes loss, backward, optimizer and transfers.
8. **Isolated backward:** fresh-graph backpropagation; excludes forward/loss preparation, gradient clearing, optimizer and transfers.
9. **Isolated inference forward:** fresh-state forward without autograd or transfers.
10. **Speed advantage:** `100 × (snnTorch time / SNNLab time − 1)`. Positive means faster, negative slower; this is a speed ratio, not a time-reduction percentage.

Pass medians measure the underlying models and do not sum to whole training
cost. These speed results apply to this workload and hardware.
