An excitatory population and an inhibitory population receive constant external
activity and connect through all four recurrent pathways. This example builds a
qualitative population model of that circuit, then decreases external drive to
inhibition to explore the resulting change in dynamics.

See the [snnlab.meanfield API Reference](/api/meanfield) for function contracts,
closure extensions and the JSON export schema.

## 1. Build the mean-field model

Start with an existing compiled network, `bundle`, and the example settings,
`cfg`. See [Defining Networks](/snnlang) for network authoring. Here the circuit
has E/I populations, all four recurrent pathways and one external input, `X`.

```python
from snnlab import lang, meanfield as mf
from snnlab.meanfield import closures
```

```python
model = mf.reduce(
    bundle,
    closure=closures.RateConductance(
        gain=closures.NoisyLIF(sigma=cfg["voltage_noise_mv"] * lang.mV),
        tau_rate=cfg["rate_relaxation_ms"],
        driving_force="resting",
    ),
    weights=mf.MeanIncomingWeights(
        cfg["mean_incoming_weights_us"],
        provenance="Explicit mean incoming conductance couplings",
    ),
    inputs={"X": mf.StationaryRate(cfg["external_rate_hz"] * lang.Hz)},
    external_filters="quasistatic",
)
```

## 2. Generated model equations

The example calls `model.write(out_dir / "model.json")` to save the model
structure, numerical parameters, assumptions and individually named equations.
This page selects those equations from the JSON document by their stable IDs.

### States

<EquationBox label="State vector: six dynamical variables">

$$
x=\left(r_{E},r_{I},g_{e}^{E},g_{e}^{I},g_{i}^{E},g_{i}^{I}\right)^{\mathsf T}
$$

</EquationBox>

The six dynamical states are $r_E,r_I$ (population firing rates in Hz) and
$g_e^E,g_e^I,g_i^E,g_i^I$ (the four recurrent conductances in µS). The subscript
$e$ or $i$ indicates excitatory or inhibitory input; the superscript identifies
the target population. A dot denotes a derivative with respect to time $t$ in ms.

### Rates

<EquationBox label="Population rates: two dynamical equations">

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{r,E}\,\dot{r_{E}} &= - r_{E} + \Phi_{E}{\left(I_{E} \right)} \\
\tau_{r,I}\,\dot{r_{I}} &= - r_{I} + \Phi_{I}{\left(I_{I} \right)}
\end{aligned}
$$

</EquationBox>

$\tau_{r,Q}$ is the population rate relaxation time in ms. The function
$\Phi_Q(I_Q)$ maps effective current $I_Q$ in nA to a stationary noisy-LIF rate
in Hz, for population $Q\in\{E,I\}$.

### Recurrent conductances

<EquationBox label="Recurrent conductances: four dynamical equations">

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{g_{e}^{E}} &= \frac{r_{E} C_{E\to E} a_{E\to E}}{1000} - \frac{g_{e}^{E}}{\tau_{E\to E}} \\
\dot{g_{e}^{I}} &= \frac{r_{E} C_{E\to I} a_{E\to I}}{1000} - \frac{g_{e}^{I}}{\tau_{E\to I}} \\
\dot{g_{i}^{E}} &= \frac{r_{I} C_{I\to E} a_{I\to E}}{1000} - \frac{g_{i}^{E}}{\tau_{I\to E}} \\
\dot{g_{i}^{I}} &= \frac{r_{I} C_{I\to I} a_{I\to I}}{1000} - \frac{g_{i}^{I}}{\tau_{I\to I}}
\end{aligned}
$$

</EquationBox>

For a pathway $P\to Q$, $C_{P\to Q}$ is the mean incoming coupling defined
below, $a_{P\to Q}$ is a dimensionless gain, and $\tau_{P\to Q}$ is its
synaptic decay time in ms. The factor 1,000 converts Hz to spikes per ms.

### External input, effective currents and state count

<EquationBox label="Stationary external input, effective currents and six-state count">

**External input**

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathrm{ext}}^{E} &= \frac{r_{X} C_{X\to E} a_{X\to E} \tau_{X\to E}}{1000} \\
g_{\mathrm{ext}}^{I} &= \frac{r_{X} C_{X\to I} a_{X\to I} \tau_{X\to I}}{1000}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Effective currents**

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{E} &= I_{\mathrm{ext},E} + \Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},E} \left(g_{e}^{E} + g_{\mathrm{ext}}^{E}\right) - \Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},E} g_{i}^{E} \\
I_{I} &= I_{\mathrm{ext},I} + \Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},I} \left(g_{e}^{I} + g_{\mathrm{ext}}^{I}\right) - \Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},I} g_{i}^{I}
\end{aligned}
$$

**State count**

Two population rates and four recurrent conductances give six dynamical
variables. E→E and I→I each have their own conductance state and differential
equation. External conductances enter as quasistatic means.

</EquationBox>

$I_{\mathrm{ext},Q}$ is applied current in nA, $r_X$ is the external source
rate in Hz, and $g_{\mathrm{ext}}^Q$ is its quasistatic mean conductance in µS.
The scheduled control changes $a_{X\to I}$; the other gains remain one.

### Fixed driving forces

<EquationBox label="Fixed excitatory and inhibitory driving forces">

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},E} &= 70.0 \\
\Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},E} &= 10.0 \\
\Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},I} &= 70.0 \\
\Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},I} &= 10.0
\end{aligned}
$$

</EquationBox>

$\Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},Q},\Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},Q}$ are fixed excitatory and
inhibitory driving forces in mV. Their products with conductance give current
in nA.

### Coupling shorthand

The coupling shorthand is also imported from the model's saved symbol glossary.
For example:

<EquationBox label="Mean incoming coupling and weight normalisation">

$$
C_{E\to I} = \frac{1}{N_{I}}\sum_{j,k}\mathbf{W}_{jk}^{E\to I}
$$

</EquationBox>

Here $C_{E\to I}$ is the mean total incoming E→I conductance jump in µS,
$N_I$ is the number of inhibitory target neurons, and
$\mathbf W_{jk}^{E\to I}$ is the effective conductance jump in µS from source
neuron $j$ to target neuron $k$. Absent connections contribute zero.
The JSON records the target count, normalisation, index meanings and units;
it does not leave the meaning of $C$ implicit.

The compact current symbols are exported from the same symbolic model;
expanding their definitions recovers the computational equations.

The same symbolic model is available for inspection and further algebra:

```python
print(mf.summary(model))
equations = mf.equations(model)  # named SymPy equations
jacobian = mf.jacobian(model)  # symbolic Jacobian
tex = mf.latex(model)
```

### Export and select an equation

```python
import json

path = model.write("model.json")
saved = json.loads(path.read_text())
rate_equation = saved["equations"]["E.rate"]["latex"]
current_equation = saved["equations"]["E.effective_current"]["latex"]
assumptions = saved["assumptions"]
coupling_definition = saved["symbols"]["E_to_I.coupling"]["definition_latex"]
```

Each equation has its own LaTeX, left and right sides, kind and symbol
dependencies. `model_definition` retains the computational expressions,
auxiliary definitions and gain specifications.
